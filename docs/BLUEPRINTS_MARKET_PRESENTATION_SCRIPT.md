# Universe Civilization: Empire At Wars

## Cover

**90 Ship Blueprints and the Player Exchange**  
A strategic fleet-design and trading system for the industrial blue/cyan universe interface  
Presenter: Universe Civilization Development Team

## Slide 1

### From hull choice to fleet doctrine

**On-slide message:** Every fleet begins with a blueprint decision.

**Speaker script:** Universe Civilization: Empire At Wars now turns fleet construction into a strategic design problem. Players choose from 90 blueprints distributed across the complete A–Z ship-class taxonomy. Each hull has a distinct role, progression tier, combat profile, logistics identity, and fitting envelope. The result is not simply a larger ship list; it is a foundation for different military doctrines.

## Slide 2

### Ninety designs across A–Z space

**On-slide message:** 26 classes, 90 hulls, multiple strategic identities.

**Speaker script:** The catalog covers classes from Assault and Bastion through Recon, Siege, Utility, Warbarge, Xenotech, Yacht, and Zenith. Every class contributes a different strategic identity. Couriers and Interceptors favor speed, Haulers and Miners favor logistics, Recon and Jammer hulls emphasize information control, while Dreadnought, Warbarge, and Zenith designs project heavy combat power.

## Slide 3

### Four roles create combined-arms fleets

**On-slide message:** Scout. Escort. Line. Command.

**Speaker script:** The catalog organizes hull variants around four practical fleet roles. Scout designs provide mobility and information. Escort hulls add protection and response capacity. Line ships deliver sustained combat pressure. Command variants support coordination and specialized fleet control. Because the roles are distributed across different classes, players can assemble fleets around doctrine rather than follow a single linear upgrade path.

## Slide 4

### Primary stats define battlefield behavior

**On-slide message:** Attack, defense, hull, shield, speed, cargo.

**Speaker script:** Primary stats establish the battlefield profile of each hull. Attack and defense shape direct combat power. Hull and shield represent durability. Speed and warp rating affect strategic movement. Cargo and capacity determine how much a fleet can carry and how effectively it can support an operation. These values feed existing shipyard, deployment, and PvP calculations.

## Slide 5

### Secondary stats reward specialization

**On-slide message:** Fitting power is more than raw damage.

**Speaker script:** The secondary stat layer adds depth beyond attack and defense. Power grid, sensor bandwidth, capacitor, crew, armor, signature, evasion, drone bandwidth, and salvage rating describe how a hull functions inside a wider fleet. A fast Recon hull may outperform a capital ship at intelligence work, while a Utility hull may recover more value after a difficult engagement.

## Slide 6

### Slot restrictions turn ships into platforms

**On-slide message:** High, medium, and low slots define the fitting envelope.

**Speaker script:** Every blueprint now exposes high-, medium-, and low-power slot counts. High slots accept weapon and siege modules. Medium slots accept shields, sensors, and electronic warfare. Low slots accept armor, cargo, and repair systems. A fitting must also remain within the hull’s power-grid, CPU, and capacitor limits. Invalid loadouts are rejected server-side before deployment or PvP launch.

## Slide 7

### Modules change the meaning of a hull

**On-slide message:** Same blueprint, different doctrine.

**Speaker script:** The module system allows a player to specialize a design without changing the underlying blueprint. Railgun Arrays, Missile Batteries, and Siege Lances increase offensive pressure. Shield Hardeners, Warp Scramblers, and Sensor Dampeners support control and defense. Cargo Optimizers, Reinforced Bulkheads, and Nanite Repair Bays improve logistics and resilience. Fitted attack, defense, and capacity are calculated from the validated loadout.

## Slide 8

### PvP begins before the battle report

**On-slide message:** Dispatch, travel, arrival, resolution.

**Speaker script:** In PvP, the fitting is part of the strategic commitment. The player selects a fleet, assigns its module loadout, validates inventory and capacity, and dispatches the force toward an enemy world. The fitting is persisted with the timed battle so the later combat resolution and battle report reflect the actual configuration sent into the theater.

## Slide 9

### The player exchange creates a second economy

**On-slide message:** Buy, sell, and trade the tools of war.

**Speaker script:** The new Player Exchange allows commanders to trade blueprint licenses and crafted modules. Market listings reserve the seller’s item, charge the buyer in Naquadah, apply a five-percent market fee, and settle the transfer transactionally. A direct-trade system supports item-for-item exchanges with optional credit offers, while pending offers reserve the proposer’s assets until acceptance, rejection, or cancellation.

## Slide 10

### Secure settlement protects the economy

**On-slide message:** Server authority, reservations, and locked transactions.

**Speaker script:** Market and trade actions are validated on the server. Item types, blueprint keys, module levels, quantities, prices, ownership, and recipient identity are checked before settlement. Listings and trade offers use row locks and transactional updates so a duplicated request cannot sell or transfer the same item twice. Rejected or cancelled offers return reserved assets to their owner.

## Slide 11

### A living ecosystem for fleet builders

**On-slide message:** Design, fit, deploy, trade, refine.

**Speaker script:** Together, the blueprint catalog, fitting rules, PvP dispatch, and player exchange create a loop for long-term progression. Players discover a design, fit it for a doctrine, deploy it in combat, recover from the result, and trade surplus blueprints or modules to fund the next strategic experiment. Industrial production and military specialization now reinforce each other.

## Slide 12

### Closing: command the design space

**On-slide message:** Build the fleet your civilization needs.

**Speaker script:** Universe Civilization: Empire At Wars is moving from a list of ships toward a complete fleet-design economy. Ninety blueprints provide the strategic vocabulary. High, medium, and low slots provide the engineering choices. PvP provides the test. The Player Exchange provides the economy. The next step is not merely to build a larger fleet, but to build the right fleet for the universe in front of you.
